# Amazon Leadership Principles

## Principles

1. Customer Obsession
Leaders start with the customer and work backwards. They work vigorously to earn and keep customer trust. Although leaders pay attention to competitors, they obsess over customers.

2. Ownership
Leaders are owners. They think long term and don't sacrifice long-term value for short-term results. They act on behalf of the entire company, beyond just their own team. They never say "that's not my job."

3. Invent and Simplify
Leaders expect and require innovation and invention from their teams and always find ways to simplify. They are externally aware, look for new ideas from everywhere, and are not limited by "not invented here."

4. Are Right, A Lot
Leaders have strong judgment and good instincts. They seek diverse perspectives and work to disconfirm their beliefs.

5. Learn and Be Curious
Leaders are never done learning and always seek to improve themselves. They are curious about new possibilities and act to explore them.

6. Hire and Develop the Best
Leaders raise the performance bar with every hire and promotion. They recognize exceptional talent, willingly move them throughout the organization, and take seriously their role in coaching others.

7. Insist on the Highest Standards
Leaders have relentlessly high standards. They are continually raising the bar and drive their teams to deliver high-quality products, services, and processes. Defects do not get sent down the line.

8. Think Big
Thinking small is a self-fulfilling prophecy. Leaders create and communicate a bold direction that inspires results. They think differently and look around corners for ways to serve customers.

9. Bias for Action
Speed matters in business. Many decisions and actions are reversible and do not need extensive study. Amazon values calculated risk-taking.

10. Frugality
Accomplish more with less. Constraints breed resourcefulness, self-sufficiency, and invention. There are no extra points for growing headcount, budget size, or fixed expense.

11. Earn Trust
Leaders listen attentively, speak candidly, and treat others respectfully. They are vocally self-critical, even when doing so is awkward or embarrassing. They benchmark themselves and their teams against the best.

12. Dive Deep
Leaders operate at all levels, stay connected to the details, audit frequently, and are skeptical when metrics and anecdote differ. No task is beneath them.

13. Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
Leaders are obligated to respectfully challenge decisions when they disagree, even when doing so is uncomfortable or exhausting. Once a decision is determined, they commit wholly.

14. Deliver Results
Leaders focus on the key inputs for their business and deliver them with the right quality and in a timely fashion. Despite setbacks, they rise to the occasion and never settle.

15. Strive to be Earth's Best Employer (added 2021)
Leaders work every day to create a safer, more productive, higher performing, more diverse, and more just work environment. They lead with empathy and ask: Are my fellow employees growing? Are they empowered?

16. Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility (added 2021)
Amazon must be humble and thoughtful about even the secondary effects of its actions. Leaders must begin each day with a determination to make better, do better, and be better for customers, employees, partners, and the world at large.


## Top 30 Behavioral Questions

Here are the **top 30 most commonly asked Amazon interview questions** mapped to the Leadership Principles:  

### Customer Obsession
**1.** Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer (internal or external). What did you do and what was the outcome?

**2.** Describe a situation where you had to make a trade-off between what a customer wanted and what was technically or operationally feasible. How did you handle it?

**3.** Tell me about a time you used customer feedback to drive a meaningful change in your product or process.

---

### Ownership
**4.** Tell me about a time you took on something significant outside your area of responsibility. Why did you do it and what happened?

**5.** Describe a time you identified a problem that wasn't your responsibility but you fixed it anyway.

**6.** Tell me about a time you made a decision that had a long-term impact, even if it wasn't the easiest short-term choice.

---

### Invent and Simplify
**7.** Tell me about a time you found a creative or innovative solution to a complex problem. How did you simplify something that was overly complicated?

**8.** Describe a time you introduced a new idea or process that improved how your team worked.

---

### Are Right, A Lot
**9.** Tell me about a time your judgment turned out to be wrong. How did you handle it and what did you learn?

**10.** Describe a situation where you had to make a decision with incomplete information. What was your approach?

**11.** Tell me about a time you sought out diverse perspectives before making an important decision. How did it change your thinking?

---

### Learn and Be Curious
**12.** Describe a time you had to learn something completely new in a short period of time. How did you approach it and what was the result?

**13.** Tell me about a time your curiosity led you to discover something that benefited your team or project.

---

### Hire and Develop the Best
**14.** Tell me about a time you mentored or coached someone on your team. What was your approach and what was the impact?

**15.** Describe a time you identified a high-potential team member and helped them grow into a bigger role.

---

### Insist on the Highest Standards
**16.** Tell me about a time you raised the bar for quality on your team. What resistance did you face and how did you overcome it?

**17.** Describe a time you refused to compromise on quality even under pressure to deliver faster.

---

### Think Big
**18.** Tell me about a time you proposed or pursued a bold, ambitious idea that others were skeptical about. What happened?

**19.** Describe a situation where thinking bigger than the immediate problem led to a better solution.

---

### Bias for Action
**20.** Tell me about a time you had to make a quick decision without all the information you needed. What did you do?

**21.** Describe a time you took a calculated risk. What was the outcome?

---

### Frugality
**22.** Tell me about a time you achieved a significant result with limited resources or budget. How did you make it work?

**23.** Describe a time you found a way to cut costs or reduce waste without sacrificing quality or outcomes.

---

### Earn Trust
**24.** Tell me about a time you had to rebuild trust with a colleague, stakeholder, or customer after something went wrong.

**25.** Describe a situation where you had to deliver difficult feedback to someone. How did you approach it?

---

### Dive Deep
**26.** Tell me about a time you used data to challenge an assumption or change a decision. How did you go about it?

**27.** Describe a situation where you had to dig deep into the details to solve a problem that others had missed.

---

### Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
**28.** Tell me about a time you strongly disagreed with your manager or team. How did you handle it, and what was the outcome?

**29.** Describe a time you committed to a decision you didn't fully agree with. How did you ensure its success?

---

### Deliver Results
**30.** Tell me about a time you had to deliver a project under a very tight deadline or with limited resources. How did you manage it?

**31.** Describe a time you faced significant obstacles while trying to achieve a goal. How did you overcome them?

## Pro Tips
- Use the **STAR format** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for every answer. 
- Prepare **3–4 strong projects** from your experience — most stories can flex across multiple principles depending on framing. 
- Amazon interviewers probe for **depth and specificity** — always be ready with metrics, exact timelines, and concrete outcomes.
